Normalisation is the process of organising a relational database to reduce
redundancy and improve data integrity. It is carried out in stages called
normal forms.

First Normal Form (1NF) requires that every column holds atomic values,
meaning each cell contains only one piece of data. There should be no
repeating groups or arrays within a single column.

Second Normal Form (2NF) builds on 1NF by eliminating partial dependencies.
Every non-key attribute must depend on the whole of the primary key, not just
part of it. This is only relevant when a composite primary key is used.

Third Normal Form (3NF) removes transitive dependencies. A non-key attribute
must not depend on another non-key attribute; it must depend directly on the
primary key. Achieving 3NF ensures that changing one piece of data does not
cause unintended changes elsewhere in the table, making the database easier
to maintain and less prone to update anomalies.
